      About

      One thought each day, from twelve paths of philosophy: Stoicism, Epicureanism, Classical Greek Philosophy, Enlightenment Ethics, Existential Thought, Humanists and Essayists, Buddhist Philosophy, Indian Philosophy, Taoism, Confucianism, Japanese Aesthetics, and Persian and Sufi Poetry.

      Each day's thought is chosen for its everyday wisdom and is usually an excerpt; "…" marks where a text has been shortened. A few older translations are lightly modernised ("thou" becomes "you"), and a few French passages are our own translations; both are noted under the thought. The source link under each thought lets you read it in full, in its own context.

      Read slowly. Sit with the question. Write if you like, in a paper notebook or here; anything you type stays on this device, and you'll find it again in past thoughts.

      Why The Thinking Place

      Philosophy began in places where people stopped to think together: the painted porch in Athens that gave the Stoics their name, Epicurus' garden, the roads Confucius walked with his students. The Thinking Place is meant to be one of those places, small enough for a pocket: a few quiet minutes each day with a thought, a question, and yourself.

        About the voices you'll find here

        The Thinking Place draws on more than two thousand years of writing. For most of that time, few women were allowed to study, publish, or be translated, so most of the texts that survive, and that are free to share today, were written by men. We have included women wherever we could, from Sei Shōnagon at the Japanese court to Mary Wollstonecraft, Émilie du Châtelet, and Simone Weil, and we will keep adding more as we find good translations.

        Your notes are yours alone

        Everything you write in The Thinking Place is stored only on this device. It is never sent to us or to anyone else. There is no account and no server copy, so no one, not even the people who run The Thinking Place, can read, recover or restore your notes.

        This also means that if you clear this browser's data, uninstall the app or lose the device, your notes are gone. Use export in Settings to keep your own copy.

        Terms & privacy

        Your notes are yours alone

        Everything you write in The Thinking Place is stored only on this device. It is never sent to us or to anyone else. There is no account and no server copy, so no one, not even the people who run The Thinking Place, can read, recover or restore your notes.

        This also means that if you clear this browser's data, uninstall the app or lose the device, your notes are gone. Use export in Settings to keep your own copy.

        What we collect

        Nothing. The Thinking Place uses no analytics, no cookies and no tracking, and we don't count our readers. Your name (if you give one), theme, sit-and-think and reminder settings are stored on your device alongside your notes. If you write to us or use the feedback form, we only see what you choose to send. The feedback form is a Google Form, so what you send there is also kept by Google under its own privacy policy; it asks for no name or email unless you choose to add one. Once loaded, the app works entirely offline.

        The texts

        Every quotation comes from a public-domain text or translation, credited under each thought with a link to its source. Where an old translation has been lightly modernised (archaic forms such as "thou" or a translator's brackets updated, the meaning unchanged), the credit says so. A few passages are our own translations from the French originals, also credited. The short notes and reflection questions are our own. The Thinking Place presents these paths side by side for personal reflection; it does not endorse or rank any of them.

        Not advice

        The Thinking Place is a space for thought, not a substitute for medical or psychological care. If you are struggling, please reach out to someone you trust or a professional.

          Where it comes from

          Every quote comes from a published translation now in the public domain, or is our own translation from the French, and is checked word for word against that text. Each thought names its translator and links to the full source, so you can read on.

          The quotes are always the translators’ own words. The notes, glossary, and maps around them were drafted with the help of AI, then checked against those same sources.

          Just you and your brain

          What you write stays on this phone. No ads, no cookies, no tracking, and free for good. Now and then, save a copy of your notes from Settings.
