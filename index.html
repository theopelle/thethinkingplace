    About

    One thought each day, the same for everyone, drawn in turn from eleven schools of thought: Stoicism, Epicureanism, Socrates and Plato, Aristotle's virtue ethics, Kant's duty ethics, Mill's utilitarianism, Existentialism, Buddhist philosophy, Taoism, Confucianism and Japanese aesthetics.

    Each day's thought is chosen for its everyday wisdom and is usually an excerpt; "…" marks where a text has been shortened. The source link under "about this thought" lets you read it in full, in its own context.

    Read slowly. Sit with the question. Write if you like, in a paper notebook or here; anything you type stays on this device, and you'll find it again in past thoughts.

    Why Many Wells

    For thousands of years, wells were where people gathered and where water was drawn up from the depths. Many thinkers have pictured wisdom the same way, as something deep that has to be drawn up with patience. Each day, Many Wells draws from a different one, from Laozi to the Stoics, and leaves you with a question to sit with. The deepest well, as Marcus Aurelius wrote, is within: "if thou wilt ever dig."

    Where the wells are

    Each dot marks where a school began. Tap one to see which.

    Confucius, the Buddha and some of the first Greek philosophers were alive in the same century, around 500 BCE. Historians call this era the Axial Age: separate wells, dug at the same time.

    Terms & privacy

    Your notes are yours alone

    Everything you write in Many Wells is stored only in this browser, on this device. It is never sent to us or to anyone else. There is no account and no server copy, so no one, not even the people who run Many Wells, can read, recover or restore your notes.

    This also means that if you clear this browser's data, uninstall the app or lose the device, your notes are gone. Use export on the About page to keep your own copy.

    What we collect

    Nothing. Many Wells uses no analytics, no cookies and no tracking. Your name (if you give one), theme and silence settings are stored on your device alongside your notes. If you report an issue or suggest a reflection, your own email app sends exactly what you choose to write, and we only see that message. Once loaded, the app works entirely offline.

    The texts

    Every quotation comes from a public-domain text or translation, credited under "about this thought" with a link to its source. The short context notes and reflection questions are our own. Many Wells presents these schools of thought side by side for personal reflection; it does not endorse or rank any of them.

    Not advice

    Many Wells is a space for thought, not a substitute for medical or psychological care. If you are struggling, please reach out to someone you trust or a professional.
